Who I am

About Me

I'm a Mechanical Engineering student at Stanford with a Computer Science minor. But honestly, I've been an engineer for as long as I can remember.

As a kid, I was obsessed with "How It's Made" and would fight my siblings for the remote just to watch factory footage. I collected Apple packaging boxes because I was fascinated by how deliberate every detail was: the material that felt perfect to touch, the seams that were clean and nearly invisible, and most of all, the way the lid would glide onto the box with that satisfying suction. Someone had deliberately engineered that friction and every single detail on that box.

I still notice these things. I rant to everyone I know about my coffee bottle: a slide lid that's completely spill-proof, an aesthetic I actually like, and a ceramic interior so the coffee doesn't taste metallic. It's clear it is such a tought-out product and I can't help but appreciate it.

This is just how my brain works. I instinctively analyze how things were made, designed, manufactured, and how they operate. I question whether I could improve a certain detail in a certain way. No one taught me to do this. It comes naturally. The difference now is that I'm learning how to actually apply it.

I work at Stanford's Hollberg Lab, where I design and build hardware for precision instrumentation. I prototype, troubleshoot, and iterate until things work the way they should. I've also worked in data engineering at CEMEX, product management at Fundación CR, and construction leadership at TECHO.

I'm curious, I pay attention, and I care about getting things right.

P.S. Outside of engineering, I bake, taught myself to crochet from YouTube, upcycle clothes, and have strong opinions about espresso beans.
